# On-Balance Volume (OBV) (Smoothed, 20)

**Version:** 11.0 (2026-08-26) — see docs/CHANGELOG.md for the canonical version history.


## 1. Introduction — Trading Function

On-Balance Volume (OBV) is a cumulative volume indicator that adds volume on up-days and subtracts volume on down-days, producing a running total that reveals whether volume is flowing into (accumulation) or out of (distribution) an asset. The OBV trendline compared against a smoothed SMA creates a flip signal. Traders use it for:
- Confirming price trends (rising OBV with rising price = healthy uptrend)
- Spotting divergences (OBV making lower highs while price makes higher highs = bearish divergence)
- Detecting smart-money accumulation/distribution before price moves

## 2. Mathematical Formula

```
OBV[i] = OBV[i-1] + Volume    (if Close[i] > Close[i-1])
OBV[i] = OBV[i-1] - Volume    (if Close[i] < Close[i-1])
OBV[i] = OBV[i-1]             (if Close[i] == Close[i-1])
OBV_SMA = SMA(OBV, smoothing_period)
```

## 3. Normalization

The normalized score in [-1, 1] is computed from OBV's distance from its smoothed baseline:

```
diff = OBV - OBV_SMA
denom = max(|OBV|, |OBV_SMA|, 1)
norm = clamp(tanh((diff / denom) × 2.5))
```

Labels: `OBV_ACCUMULATION` (norm > 0.1), `OBV_DISTRIBUTION` (norm < -0.1), `OBV_NEUTRAL`.

## 4. Signals

| SignalKind | Label Pattern | Trigger Condition | Direction |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|-----------|
| TrendFlip | OBV_TREND_FLIP_BULLISH | OBV crosses above its SMA (transition-only). Accumulation trend flipping from distribution. | Bullish |
| TrendFlip | OBV_TREND_FLIP_BEARISH | OBV crosses below its SMA (transition-only). Distribution trend flipping from accumulation. | Bearish |
| Divergence | BULLISH_DIVERGENCE | Price makes lower low, OBV makes higher low — hidden accumulation. Detected via 20-bar SeriesDivergence. **Confirmed when (a) a candle close breaks below the nearest Support level and immediately reverses back above it (liquidity sweep / reclaim), OR (b) a candle close breaks above the nearest Resistance level (structural break); the tolerance buffer is 0.2% of level price.** | Bullish |
| Divergence | BEARISH_DIVERGENCE | Price makes higher high, OBV makes lower high — distribution exhaustion. **Confirmed when (a) a candle close breaks above the nearest Resistance level and immediately reverses back below it (liquidity sweep / reclaim), OR (b) a candle close breaks below the nearest Support level (structural break); tolerance 0.2%.** | Bearish |

> Registry manifest (`signal_types`): Divergence, TrendFlip. The legacy `Threshold` rows (`OBV_ACCUMULATION` / `OBV_DISTRIBUTION` / `OBV_NEUTRAL`) were removed — neither the registry nor the runtime deriver emits them.

**Divergence Lifecycle:** Potential (oscillator disagrees with price, no structural break) → Confirmed (a candle close decisively sweeps / reclaims or breaks through the relevant S/R boundary by >0.2% tolerance). Potential divergences are secondary confluence only — do not trade them as primary signals. The divergence is emitted as a nested `Divergence` signal on the `obv` key — there is no separate `obv_divergence` registry entry or JSON key.

> **Wording precision (correction).** A previous version of this table said *"Potential → Confirmed when nearest Support level broken with 0.2% tolerance"* for the bullish signal. A *sustained downward break* of support is a **bearish structural breakdown** — it cannot confirm a **bullish reversal** divergence. The corrected confirmation rule admits two regimes: a *sweep / reclaim* (the canonical bullish trigger: price dips under support and closes back above) **or** a structural break above resistance (the canonical bullish trend change). The bear-side mirrors this.
>
> This rule is now consistent with [04-02-11-rsi.md §Confirmation Criteria](./04-02-11-rsi.md), where the equivalent sweep / reclaim pattern is spelled out explicitly.

## 5. Scoring

`obv` is `directional: true`. The OBV trendline flip captures smart-money flow shifts; the divergence signal detects exhaustion. The divergence is a nested `Divergence` signal on the `obv` key, not a separate registry entry.
